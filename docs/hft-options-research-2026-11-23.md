# HFT and options research for NIFTY

23 November 2026 · Research and implementation proposal · Platform checkout `d80eb3a`

## 1. Executive summary

**Develop futures order-flow imbalance first.** The engine already records changes in the near-month NIFTY future’s displayed book. Today its 20-level signal reads one book per minute, and its futures trade-flow signal estimates who initiated trades from volume and quotes. A new feature can measure the *changes* in displayed supply and demand throughout each minute. Test whether it adds anything to the current book lean and breadth at the existing 15-minute NIFTY horizon. The case for testing it is strong. Its directional and after-cost value on NIFTY is unknown. [Current book and trade-flow signals](../src/lib/insight/pulse.ts) · [Existing minute summaries](../src/engine/journal/side-series.ts) · [Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402)

**Use constituent breadth to confirm direction and option prices to decide whether that direction is worth buying.** Breadth is already in the engine, making it a useful control and a cheap second experiment. A parity-based implied-volatility and break-even estimate can then assess whether buying a call or put leaves enough potential gain after spread, time decay and charges. Keep the three effects measurable on their own before combining them. [Prediction scoreboard](predictions.md) · [Current option chain analytics](../src/lib/insight/chain.ts) · [Decision API pricing inputs](../src/engine/trading/decision.ts)

**Treat next-tick signals as diagnostics through Dhan.** A quoted book imbalance can forecast a price change that is over before an order arrives. The platform has broker snapshots and local receive times. Institutional queue trading depends on exchange event data, execution time measurement and queue information. A 20- or 200-level book adds price levels. It cannot recover order IDs, original event order or our position in the queue. Market making, multi-leg spreads and trading futures also require a constitution amendment. [Dhan market-depth format](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/full-market-depth/) · [NSE real-time data products](https://www.nseindia.com/static/market-data/real-time-data-subscription) · [NSE co-location](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/platform-services-co-location-facility) · [Constitution](constitution.md)

**What the local evidence supports.** The checkout contains four dates with depth files and three with chain files. The existing scoreboard’s first published results have two sessions with priced options. Breadth beat the same-minute coin flip on both, static book lean on one, and the existing futures trade-flow rule on neither. The separate 23 November prod report describes one profitable breadth day on an unusually strong down day and no orders reaching Dhan. Candidate performance and order execution latency remain unmeasured. [Recorded data](#5-data-gap-analysis) · [First scoreboard results](predictions.md#first-results) · [23 November prod report](data-report-2026-11-23.md)

## 4. Research findings

### Displayed imbalance, order flow and microprice

- **Order book imbalance (OBI)** is the difference in displayed depth across the two sides. For a level count `L`, compute `I_L = Σ wₗ(bidQtyₗ − askQtyₗ) / Σ wₗ(bidQtyₗ + askQtyₗ)`, with positive weights and a nonzero denominator. Rank weights such as `1/L` for equal weighting or `1/l` for declining weighting are simple controls. A fixed price band in ticks around the midpoint is another option when spreads differ. Report top-of-book alongside deep values because distant resting size can swamp the price levels that matter now. Gould and Bonart studied the direction of the next mid-price change in ten liquid Nasdaq stocks and found stronger effects in large-tick stocks. Thin NIFTY strikes, changes in spread, and large orders that disappear before trading may all erode the result. [Gould and Bonart](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492)
- **Order flow imbalance (OFI)** adds signed changes in displayed queues. At unchanged prices, bid additions and ask reductions add positive pressure. A best-price change is handled using the old or new queue, as defined in the first deep dive. The extension across levels is multi-level OFI. Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov found a roughly linear *contemporaneous* relationship between OFI and mid-price moves across 50 US equities, with impact larger in shallow books. Xu, Gould and Howison improved out-of-sample fit for a contemporaneous mid-price change on six Nasdaq equities by using deeper levels. Cont, Cucuringu and Zhang separately reported short-lived *forward* value from lagged cross-asset OFI. These are different targets and none establishes forward returns for NIFTY options. [Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402) · [Xu, Gould and Howison](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06230) · [Cont, Cucuringu and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13213)
- **Signed traded volume** is a separate feature. Compare a new last trade price to the preceding eligible quote and assign a buy or sell sign only when the match is clear. A cumulative volume jump can contain multiple trades, so quote and tick rules yield an estimate. Keep unknown volume and agreement with snapshot OFI visible. Near-ATM aggregate call and put pressure by expiry and comparable moneyness can confirm direction or show a repricing. A buyer-initiated put can be a hedge, and rising open interest alone cannot identify buyer or seller intent. [Engine futures trade-flow estimator](../src/engine/journal/session-summary.ts) · [Dhan Full packet](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/live-market-feed/)
- **Weighted mid and microprice.** For bid `b`, ask `a` and top sizes `q_b, q_a`, `weighted_mid = (a q_b + b q_a)/(q_b+q_a) = mid + ((a−b)/2) I₁`. A large bid size nudges it up toward the ask. Stoikov’s learned microprice estimates the price adjustment conditional on spread and imbalance. If `Q` is the within-mid transition matrix, `R` the matrix of transitions to price changes `k`, and `B` the transition matrix between book states at a mid-price change, the first-change adjustment is `g₁ = (I−Q)⁻¹Rk` and further expected changes can be added as `Σ Bʲg₁`. Binning, warm-up, regime stability and out-of-sample estimation matter. The weighted-mid version needs no fitting and is the right diagnostic to start with. Use an option’s weighted mid to measure likely adverse entry, with the executable ask as the actual entry reference. [Stoikov paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2970694) · [Author notebook](https://github.com/sstoikov/microprice/blob/4e5f29a843cb59f82bf3c64b0725642792048124/Microprice%20-%20Big%20Data%20Conference.ipynb)

### Cross-section, lead–lag and statistical arbitrage

- **Breadth and basket divergence.** Construct `R_basket = Σ wᵢrᵢ` and a breadth score `Σ wᵢ sign(rᵢ)` from fixed, as-of windows. Compare the basket with the published index and futures mid on the same receive-time grid. Measure a leader’s incremental forecast over lagged NIFTY moves, breadth and time-of-day. Beware that the cash prints can reach us at different delays and a published index can update after its inputs for mechanical reasons. A lead that disappears after aligning freshness or applying a delay cannot justify taking the option spread. Official NIFTY weighting is free-float adjusted. The repo’s Dhan snapshot gives full market-cap shares, and Pulse can estimate effective weights from the session prefix. Use degree of coverage and weight provenance as features. [NSE index methodology](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/Method_NIFTY_Equity_Indices.pdf) · [Pulse weighting code](../src/lib/insight/pulse.ts) · [Hasbrouck on US equity-index price discovery](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1540-6261.2003.00609.x)
- **Futures basis and synthetic forward.** `F−S` includes financing, dividends, time to expiry and index staleness. An indicative carry-adjusted residual is `F − S exp((r−q)T)` for rate `r`, dividend yield `q` and matched maturity `T`. For a weekly option, construct its own forward from same-strike call/put quotes. A monthly futures quote is useful for movement and as a cross-check with a maturity adjustment. Track futures, cash basket and synthetic-forward residuals as lead–lag features. A lag regression or error-correction test can ask whether `ΔS` follows `F−S` conditional on current moves, with the coefficient estimated only on prior complete sessions. Expiry rolls, auctions, spread bounds and March-to-November changes in fee schedules can dominate small residuals. An apparent parity violation is actionable as an arbitrage only if synchronized executable legs, margin and fees support it. The present mandate uses residuals to inform a single call or put. [Buccheri, Corsi and Peluso on lead–lag](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/23591/1/BuccheriCorsiPeluso_MLA_submitted_Nov2019.pdf) · [Black forward pricing](https://doi.org/10.1016%2F0304-405x%2876%2990024-6)

### Options volatility and relative value

- **Black-76 and Greeks.** For expiry year fraction `T`, forward `F`, strike `K`, discount `D`, and volatility `σ`, set `d₁=[ln(F/K)+σ²T/2]/(σ√T)` and `d₂=d₁−σ√T`. The call is `D[FΦ(d₁)−KΦ(d₂)]` and the put `D[KΦ(−d₂)−FΦ(−d₁)]`. Solve for IV from a price inside model bounds. Delta measures response to the underlying or forward according to the convention, gamma is curvature, vega is IV sensitivity, and theta is time decay. Check units and reprice through time instead of depending on a first-order Greek near expiry. Spot and forward deltas, annual volatility clocks, and vega per one volatility point versus per unit can differ. [Black 1976](https://doi.org/10.1016%2F0304-405x%2876%2990024-6) · [Existing pricing](../src/lib/insight/derivatives.ts)
- **Smile and term structure.** Use ATM total variance `w=σ²T`, skew at fixed log-forward moneyness `k=ln(K/F)` or fixed delta, and changes at the same strike and expiry. An SVI slice fits `w(k)=a+b[ρ(k−m)+√((k−m)²+s²)]`. In a fuller study, weight reliable narrow-spread quotes, enforce butterfly and calendar constraints and compare executable prices with a fit that leaves the contract under test out. A stale or wide option can look unusually cheap in IV. A surface fit or positive residual can help value a selected long option. Trading surface convergence with spreads, delta-hedged variance or a short iron condor would change the mandate. A short condor sells the inner call and put and buys the outer wings, so apparent premium capture hides four spreads and legging risk. [Gatheral and Jacquier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.0646) · [Repo chain analytics](../src/lib/insight/chain.ts) · [Condor payoff source](https://github.com/joaquinbejar/OptionStratLib/blob/0cf8223d1ec638c29df93e660983c13104cb41f2/crates/optionstratlib-strategies/src/strategies/iron_condor.rs)

### Market making, execution and liquidity shocks

- **Avellaneda–Stoikov.** With mid `S`, signed inventory `q`, risk aversion `γ`, price variance rate `σ²`, time left `τ` and exponential arrival intensity `A exp(−κδ)` at quote distance `δ`, a commonly used symmetric approximation gives reservation price `r=S−qγσ²τ` and full spread `γσ²τ+2 ln(1+γ/κ)/γ`. Quote `r ± spread/2` after tick and risk constraints. Long inventory pulls both quotes down to encourage sales. GLFT imposes inventory bounds and yields a related inventory-skewed quote policy. Fits to trade distance or one’s own quote fills depend on liquidity, queue priority and regime. An option market maker also holds delta, gamma and vega risk and requires hedge capacity. Those data, executions and permissions are outside this experiment. [Avellaneda and Stoikov](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf) · [Guéant, Lehalle and Fernandez-Tapia](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3115)
- **Execution.** For one lot, compare crossing with a bounded limit against waiting, using expected net value `P(fill) × E(net | fill)` plus a value for the missed trade and any exit costs of partial fills. Measure implementation shortfall from decision-time mid in rupees, side adjusted, and measure subsequent mid moves following fills to expose adverse selection. A passive buy can look cheap precisely when prices fall through it. Lo, MacKinlay and Zhang use actual order histories to estimate time to execution. Almgren–Chriss traces a cost/risk trade-off for splitting a portfolio order over time. For this platform the closest application is a one-lot entry/exit policy and sensitivity to routing delay, with no parent-order slicing. [Lo, MacKinlay and Zhang](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w6257/w6257.pdf) · [Almgren and Chriss](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5df018ec4c9d61288094246c/t/5e39db229a16b072d97fce9b/1580849954431/Optimal%2BExecution%2Bof%2BPortfolio%2BTransaction%2B_%2BAlmgren%26Chriss%2B1999.pdf)
- **Liquidity shocks and replenishment.** Track top-depth depletion, subsequent refill, spread widening and OFI sign persistence. Queue-reactive models use add, cancel and market order intensities conditional on the queue state and are useful for understanding the failure cases of a fill simulator. Snapshot recovery and persistent pressure can be observed here. Separate intensities for actual actions cannot be estimated reliably without an event feed. [Huang, Lehalle and Rosenbaum](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.0563) · [Moallemi and Yuan on queue position](https://moallemi.com/ciamac/papers/queue-value-2016.pdf)

### Deep learning

DeepLOB inputs a time-by-level tensor of prices and quantities. Convolutions and parallel filters learn book structure and local temporal patterns, and an LSTM classifies the next horizon’s mid-price move. Kolm, Turiel and Westray found that models on stationary order-flow inputs often outperformed models on raw books across 115 Nasdaq stocks, with an effective stock-specific forecast horizon about two average price changes. Briola and coauthors examine how accurate classifications translate into fewer realizable transactions once trade paths are considered. The existing recordings can test feature extraction and labels. There are too few independent sessions to defend a fitted neural model. A future comparison starts with a regularized linear input ablation, fixes the horizon and splits by day before creating overlapping windows. [DeepLOB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03668) · [Deep order-flow imbalance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/mafi.12413) · [Deep limit order book forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09267)

## 9. Top three algorithm deep dives

### 1. Futures snapshot OFI and within-minute pressure

**Question.** Do changes in the near NIFTY future’s displayed book add information beyond its most recent book, recent price changes and traded-volume imbalance? This question is answerable with the raw recordings. It is the largest unused source of data among the three proposals. The expected value as a 15-minute feature is a hypothesis.

**Inputs and math.** At each valid consecutive snapshot and level, let `b,a` be bid and ask prices and `q_b,q_a` their sizes. A subscript `prev` means the preceding valid snapshot. `1[condition]` is one when true and zero otherwise.

```text
bid_flow = 1[b >= b_prev] * q_b - 1[b <= b_prev] * q_b_prev
ask_flow = 1[a <= a_prev] * q_a - 1[a >= a_prev] * q_a_prev
OFI_increment = bid_flow - ask_flow
```

If a bid at the same price grows from 200 to 240 units and an ask moves up, removing the old best ask of 120 units, this measure adds `+40 +120 = +160`. It measures the price/size transition visible to us. A batched snapshot cannot determine how much of the old ask traded or was pulled. For each level aggregate the increments over the window and divide by its average total displayed size. For a first multi-level feature, combine the five normalized flows with weights proportional to `1/l`. Compute a 20-level version as a fixed comparison. Changing level ranks can move existing size into or out of the window. Track price-keyed change at shared prices as a diagnostic and avoid interpreting a tail level leaving coverage as a cancellation. [OFI definition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402) · [Multi-level formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06230)

**First candidate.** Record the following fields each minute. A full minute of input and its freshest usable book are explicit requirements. A starting protocol could mark book sides stale after two seconds and require at least 45 seconds of valid coverage within the minute. Confirm those assumptions against development-day data and freeze them before the first unseen session. Keep both the raw-frame and a fixed one-second sampling diagnostic to expose whether apparent signal power comes from high packet counts.

```text
At session start choose the front NIFTY future by point-in-time metadata
On a depth frame collect both sides for that contract
On disconnect, restart or contract change clear the prior book and window
For each valid complete book in receive-time order
    Record valid time coverage and the latest static OBI and weighted mid
    Add OFI from the preceding book only within the same valid segment
At minute cutoff output OFI(1), weighted OFI(5), weighted OFI(20), OBI(1/5/20),
    weighted-mid shift in ticks, spread, side ages, coverage and recent return
Emit a standalone direction from sign(OFI(5)) when coverage is sufficient
Emit a second direction only when that sign agrees with existing breadth
```

Track contemporaneous impact for comparison with the literature. For forecasts, measure future futures-mid change at 5, 15, 30 and 60 seconds from the cutoff and future NIFTY change at 15 minutes, with labels strictly after feature collection. Compare static OBI, prior return, signed trade flow, L1 OFI and 5-/20-level OFI on the same times. Check whether 20 levels improve over five and whether confirmation with breadth adds value to breadth alone. Measure effect by liquidity, roll/expiry and time of day. Where a valid test call becomes a long option in the paper policy, fix the call/put contract at the first eligible quote, allow the configured arrival delay and grade one-lot rupees. Drop subsecond order entry if the signal decays before the earliest credible fill. ### 2. Weighted breadth and futures confirmation

**Question.** Does the distribution of stock moves, with fresh futures confirmation, predict more than the existing count-based breadth rule or the move already visible in NIFTY? Futures and the published index must be aligned causally, especially around the open and contract roll.

**Inputs and outputs.** Use timestamped stock quotes, the NIFTY index, near-futures mid, prior-close references and the as-of constituent set and weights. For fresh stock `i`, calculate `rᵢ = ln(pᵢ(t)/pᵢ(t−5m))`. With original weights summing to one, `coverage = Σ valid wᵢ`, `Wᵢ=wᵢ/coverage`, `B₅=Σ Wᵢ sign(rᵢ)` and `R₅=Σ Wᵢ rᵢ`. The five-minute basket residual `R₅−ln(NIFTY(t)/NIFTY(t−5m))` measures how far the basket has moved relative to the published index. Record the percentage of index weight with eligible quotes, the largest contributors, the futures return, and the residual and vote. The starting causal rule can use the checked-in proxy weights in shadow if official weights are unavailable. Freeze them for the run and mark provenance. [NIFTY weight methodology](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/Method_NIFTY_Equity_Indices.pdf) · [Local weight source](../src/lib/index-constituents.ts) · [Current breadth and basket controls](../src/lib/insight/pulse.ts)

```text
At t and t minus five minutes select each instrument's last eligible quote
Discard stocks with missing or stale endpoints and report original weight coverage
Require coverage of at least 90 percent as an initial research assumption
Compute B5, R5, the index residual and the futures return over the same window
Candidate A uses sign(B5) if it agrees with sign(R5)
Candidate B uses the same direction only if the futures return also agrees
Log the residual separately and emit a reason if coverage or agreement fails
```

Use existing breadth, trend from open, recent index return, futures return and basket lead as single-feature controls. Compare A to current breadth, then B to A at shared timestamps and across all opportunities. Compare results with equal weights, as-of free-float weights where available and cash mids where those dates contain Full data. Run a timestamp sensitivity check that delays the fastest series or uses only fresh observations from both endpoints. Compare positive and negative lag correlations as a diagnostic for publication delay, then measure directional lift and option rupees using strictly forward horizons. Test expiry and non-expiry days separately. Evaluate a BANKNIFTY lead only after accounting for overlap in its constituents and for whether NIFTY futures already moved. A big financial-sector move can dominate both series and create an apparent extra vote. [NSE weighting methodology](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/Method_NIFTY_Equity_Indices.pdf) · [Index price discovery study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1540-6261.2003.00609.x)

### 3. Parity-based IV and the long-option break-even move

**Question.** For the same direction and holding period, does a quote-based break-even estimate identify which calls or puts pay for their spread, time decay and volatility risk? This is a contract or abstention decision after a directional forecast. The simplest experiment keeps the same contract policy on both sides of the comparison.

**Inputs and outputs.** Use bid/ask and size for the chosen long option, time remaining to expiry, the discount factor `D`, synchronized liquid same-expiry call/put pairs and a versioned forecast of the underlying move. For each near-ATM `K`, compute `F_K=K+(call_mid−put_mid)/D`. Its executable interval is `[K+(call_bid−put_ask)/D, K+(call_ask−put_bid)/D]`. Take a robust center over valid tight intervals and report disagreement. The current repo function approximates `D=1`, uses the spot-nearest ATM pair and may fall back to last trade. For strict economic grading retain the two-sided price and width, turn down stale or incoherent pairs, and keep clock and units consistent across baseline and forecast. Add optional skew and total-variance context by fixed moneyness. The current proposal-time Decision API returns a judgment and quality score. Take the break-even move to that gate immediately in shadow. Testing a numeric forecast-versus-hurdle rule needs a fixed ex-ante move estimate, for example from the proposed timed prediction, or a predeclared scenario based on observed past returns. The same current directional calls serve as the control. [Existing chain pricing](../src/lib/insight/chain.ts) · [Current Decision API questions](../src/engine/trading/decision.ts) · [Forward pricing](https://doi.org/10.1016%2F0304-405x%2876%2990024-6) · [Pricing implementation reference](https://github.com/lballabio/QuantLib/blob/26a44f1fc884c71f16c9d5c0e505f82349609088/ql/pricingengines/blackformula.cpp)

```text
Fix the signal direction, 15-minute hold and existing ATM selection policy
Read same-expiry chain and Full option quotes as of decision time
Compute forward and IV with quote ages, parity interval and bound checks
Set buy to executable ask plus modeled slippage
For the forecast move and declared IV scenarios
    Reprice the same strike at the shorter remaining maturity
    Convert the model value to a future bid using spread and slippage assumptions
    Calculate net rupees for the recorded lot after date-effective charges
Solve for the directional forward move giving zero net at unchanged IV
Emit break-even move, base and stressed net rupees, and missing-data reasons
In shadow compare the original call with a policy taking only positive base net
```

For an audit example with a 65-unit lot, assume mid ₹100, ask ₹101 and an eventual exit spread of ₹2, with no additional slippage. A forecast `ΔF=+18` and call delta `0.5` add ₹9 to the premium per unit. If time decay takes ₹1.30 and a two-point IV decrease takes ₹1.60, the estimated exit mid is ₹106.10 and the estimated bid is ₹105.10, before gamma. The modeled lot earns ₹266.50 before fees and about ₹203 after about ₹63 in charges using the repo rates. With no move or IV change and the same decay, the exit bid is ₹97.70 and the after-cost result is about −₹277. This arithmetic illustrates why a directional hit can be too small. A decision near expiry calls for full repricing, stressed IV/spread paths and the actual observed bid at exit to grade it. [Shared fee calculation](../src/lib/trading/charges.ts) · [Existing break-even inputs](../src/engine/trading/decision.ts)

Validate against identical ungated directional calls on fresh quotes. Compare candidate-available rates, rejected-trade P&L, same-minute paired P&L, opportunity-level P&L and downside protection by expiry and IV regime. Use a spread-only gate as a control to see whether model IV and theta add anything. Compare predicted break-even and P&L with realized values, and separate directional error from IV change and execution shortfall. Keep unknown IV or stale quotes as explicit abstentions for the pricing candidate. If it works only on expiry days, or only under unchanged IV and ideal spreads, more evidence is needed before it can change the paper policy.
